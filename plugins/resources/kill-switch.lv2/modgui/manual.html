Kill Switch chops your signal in a rhythm, like the momentary kill switch some guitars have: while the plugin is on, a looping pattern lets the sound through or cuts it, in time with the tempo you set. Bypassed, your signal is clean.

You pick one of 10 patterns (from a plain chop to a machine gun), set the tempo and the speed, and then use Depth and Edge to decide how deep and how hard the cuts are. It is one mono audio effect, with a footswitch on the plugin that is also its bypass.

01

Quick start

You need a pedalboard with an audio input and an output, and a guitar (or any sound) going through it.

  1. Add Kill Switch to your pedalboard and connect it between your guitar input and the rest of the chain (or between the amp and the output). It has one audio input and one audio output.
  2. Turn it on. The plugin starts on (the red light over the footswitch is lit and the label says On): play a note and it is chopped at once. Click the footswitch to bypass it: the sound is clean again.

  3. Choose a pattern. Use the arrows on each side of its name. The squares under the name show the pattern: a lit square lets the signal through, a dark one cuts it.

  4. Set the tempo with − and + (hold one to go on), and pick the speed (½x, 1x, 2x, 4x): the same pattern at half, normal, double or four times its speed, without touching the tempo.

  5. Shape the cut. Depth is how much of the signal a cut removes (100% is silence) and Edge is how long it takes to close and open (short is a hard, percussive chop; long is a soft swell).

  6. Put it under your foot. Assign the plugin's footswitch (its Bypass) to a device footswitch, in Momentary mode if you want the effect on only while you hold it, as with the switch on a guitar (section 4).

Every pattern starts the moment you switch the plugin on

Whenever the plugin goes from bypassed to on, the pattern starts again from its first step, and all of the patterns begin with a cut, as if you had pressed the kill switch right then. Press the footswitch in time with the beat and the chops fall where you want them.

03

The patterns

Each pattern is a loop of steps. In every step the signal is either let through or cut; the plugin goes from one step to the next in time with the tempo, and from the last step back to the first. The grids show every pattern as sixteen steps (the shorter ones repeat): a coloured square lets the signal through and a grey one cuts it.

Generic grooves

Six patterns that fit any style. Chop 8ths and Chop 16ths are the plain on-off chop at those note values, Triplet chop does it in triplets, Machine gun is the same at thirty-second notes, Syncopated 3-3-2 is the Latin-flavoured grouping of eight steps (three, three and two) and Gallop is a short-short-long figure of four.

Inspired by guitarists famous for the kill switch

Four longer, busier patterns named after players known for the kill switch. The rhythms are our approximation, not a transcription of anyone's playing: use them as a starting point and change the tempo, the speed, the depth and the edge until it fits your song.

Steps per beat: Morello solo chop 4 · Morello toggle solo 4 · Greenwood chunk 2 · Buckethead burst 8

The tempo says how many beats there are in a minute, and every pattern has its own number of steps per beat (the line under each grid), so the length of a step is 60 / (tempo × steps per beat × speed) seconds. At 120 BPM, Chop 8ths has a step of 250 ms and Machine gun a step of 62.5 ms.

The tempo is the plugin's own

Kill Switch does not follow the tempo of the pedalboard or of a MIDI clock: set the tempo here, or assign it to a knob or to an expression pedal.

04

Playing it live

Every control of the plugin can be assigned to a device control (a footswitch, a knob, an expression pedal), the way you assign any parameter: open the settings of the plugin (the gear icon) and click the control to assign.

Control Good to assign it to
Bypass A footswitch. In the dialog choose Momentary On to have the effect on only while you hold the footswitch (the kill switch of a guitar: you press, it chops; you let go, it is clean), or leave it as a toggle to turn it on and off with each press.
Pattern A knob, or a footswitch that goes through the patterns.
Tempo A knob, or an expression pedal.
Speed A footswitch: hold it for double speed, let go for normal.
Depth A knob or an expression pedal: from a gentle tremolo to a full cut.
Edge A knob: from a hard chop to a soft swell.

The plugin shows nothing on the device screen, so there is no pop-up to set up.

06

Tips

Set the tempo to the song, then leave it

The patterns are in time with the tempo you set, not with the band. Adjust the number until the chops sit with the drums: a difference of one or two BPM is enough to drift off the beat over a bar.

Switch it on on the beat

The pattern restarts from its first step every time you switch the plugin on, and every pattern starts with a cut. Press the footswitch on the beat (or just before it) and the first chop lands exactly there.

Soften it with Depth

At 100% a cut is silence. At 40 to 60% the signal only dips, which sounds like a sharp tremolo, and fits a sound that should stay present (an ambient pad, a drive that you do not want to disappear).

Use Edge to change the character

Short edges (below 1 ms) are percussive: the sound is stopped as if a switch were flicked. Long edges (10 ms and more) turn the chop into a soft swell.

Put it where you want the cut

Before a delay, the cut chops what goes into the delay and the repeats go on ringing; after the delay, the repeats are chopped too. Try both places.

07

Questions & answers

Nothing is cut, I hear my guitar as it is.

Check that the label at the top of the plugin says On and the red light above the footswitch is lit. If it says Bypassed, click the footswitch. If the footswitch is assigned to a device control in Momentary mode, the effect is on only while you hold it.

The chops are not on the beat of my song.

The tempo is the plugin's own (section 3): set it by ear, or assign it to a knob. And switch the effect on on the beat: the pattern starts again from the beginning each time.

Does it add latency? Is it stereo?

No latency: the signal is only multiplied by a number that goes up and down. The plugin is mono: one audio input and one output. On a stereo pedalboard, put one on each side with the same pattern and tempo.

I hear a click at each cut.

Make Edge longer. At 0.1 ms the signal is stopped almost at once, which can click on a low note.

Does it change the level of my sound when it is on?

Not while a step lets the signal through (it passes unchanged): the level only goes down in the cuts. So there is nothing to compensate with a volume control.

Why are the patterns named after guitarists?

They are inspired by guitarists known for the kill switch, as a way to find the rhythm: the steps are our approximation of the style, not a transcription, and the plugin has no connection with the players or with their songs.
